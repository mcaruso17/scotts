## Key Points / Insights

- The new book, *Causal Inference: the Remix*, is ~800 pages and releases **25 August 2026** with Yale University Press.
- Cunningham's stated goal is to be "the best **second book** on the syllabus" — a supportive complement to Angrist–Pischke and Wooldridge rather than the primary text.
- His model is **Peter Kennedy's *A Guide to Econometrics***, a beloved intuition-building companion that went through six editions.
- **Unchanged material:** most DAG content (sample-selection trimmed) and ~90–95% of the potential-outcomes chapter (with new empirical examples, including a Giffen-good paper by Jensen & Miller).
- **Biggest revisions** are to the unconfoundedness and panel-data chapters; the probability/regression chapter was dropped and regression folded into unconfoundedness (motivating the "why not just run a regression?" thread and heterogeneous effects / common support / extrapolation).
- **New synthetic-control coverage:** augmented synth, lightly penalized (ridge) regression, matrix completion, and synthetic diff-in-diff.
- The **narrative spine** traces causal inference through two streams — Orley Ashenfelter's Princeton credibility tradition and Don Rubin's Harvard potential-outcomes framework — bridged by Josh Angrist and Guido Imbens (the "LATE" lineage).
- Cunningham defends **Rubin's central role**, calling the elevation of Roy over Rubin "revisionist history."
- His deeper aim: use personal stories so readers who aren't from the Princeton–Cambridge labor tradition feel they **belong** to the "credibility revolution."
