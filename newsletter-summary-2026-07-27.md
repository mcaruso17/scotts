**Author:** Scott Cunningham (Scott's Mixtape Substack — "Claude Code" series)

## Main themes

- **Mistakes as human capital** — whether the errors we make in research are, paradoxically, part of how we grow as researchers.
- **AI agents and error prevention** — if Claude Code can catch the coding errors that pollute the scientific record, should it?
- **The two ledgers** — cleaner *output* (intrinsically valuable) vs. the *personal growth* that comes from making, and suffering, mistakes.
- **Stakes and learning** — a running poker analogy: you only truly learn when something is on the line.

## Key points / insights

- Scott recounts a Stata coding error found only at the R&R stage that flipped a paper from "significant and interesting" to "not," and which he never resubmitted — for 10+ years it sat at the journal awaiting his revision.
- The lasting lessons weren't syntax but *practice*: separating raw from cleaned data, pipelined code that auto-builds tables/figures, version control, and the danger of "liking your results."
- That episode is where he first learned scientific integrity as an explicit norm — something classrooms and mentorship can't easily teach.
- **Pro-agent side of the ledger:** for every caught error there are ~20 uncaught ones that get published, so agents that surface errors would, in aggregate, improve the scientific record.
- **The open question:** if progress depends on *accuracy now* but also on *human-capital accumulation later*, do we actually want Claude Code to remove our ability to make mistakes?
- **Stakes change behavior:** like playing poker for real vs. pretend money, you learn from bad beats *and* good beats because something is at risk — remove all threat of pain and you can become "lazy and haphazard."
- He deliberately reaches no firm conclusion: mistakes matter (that's why we wear seat belts), yet a life with every mistake prevented may forfeit the failure-driven knowledge that can't be gained any other way.
