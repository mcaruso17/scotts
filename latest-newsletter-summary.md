## Main topics / themes
- **Continuous difference-in-differences** — the ongoing 15-episode series estimating the effect of Texas House Bill 2 (which closed half the state's abortion clinics) on marriage rates.
- **The parallel trends assumption** — the identifying assumption behind any diff-in-diff estimate.
- **Confounders and robustness** — specifically, whether an oil / fracking / energy shock could be biasing the estimates.

## Key points / insights
- Scott Cunningham and co-author Caitlin Myers have studied HB2's effect on marriage rates for 14 straight episodes using continuous diff-in-diff.
- Every diff-in-diff estimate rests on a parallel trends assumption, which this episode stress-tests.
- The suspected confounder is energy extraction technology (fracking): booms can bring men into communities temporarily for work rather than permanent settlement.
- Male in-migration "men in transit" can, on its own, reshape local marriage markets.
- If such shocks hit treatment and control counties differently, parallel trends could be *mechanically* violated.
- Crucially, parallel trends concerns differential effects on **outcome trends**, and a violation can come through **either** the treatment **or** the control group.
- Research assistant "Hannah" investigated the fracking-confounder question and reports her findings in the episode.
