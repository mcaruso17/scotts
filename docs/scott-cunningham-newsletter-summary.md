## Main themes

- **Agent provenance / reasoning trails** — Claude Code can keep a diary of what
  it did in a project, *including abandoned work and the reasons for choices*.
- **AI agents for empirical research** — treating the agent as a fast, fallible
  research assistant directed in prose rather than code.
- **Live, public research** — ideating and writing a real paper end-to-end on
  video (the Myers collaboration).
- **Research vs. publishing divergence** — production is getting cheap;
  credible publication and verification are the new scarce goods.

## Key points (one line each)

- Claude Code logs a recoverable record of its work, including dead-ends, which
  is useful provenance a git history alone doesn't capture.
- Cunningham learned of the "reasons diary" from a grad student (Theo) after an
  MIT talk — a reminder the tooling's affordances are still being discovered.
- The series repeatedly stresses **independent verification**: fresh sessions,
  separate replication scripts, and cross-package audits over trusting output.
- Earlier installment ("Claude Code 27") argues doing research and *publishing*
  it are decoupling, raising "paper-milling" and peer-review strain.
- The comparative advantage of human researchers is moving to judgement —
  posing questions, credible identification, and vouching for results.
- The work is packaged into reusable tooling (Referee 2, Blindspot, Bibcheck)
  so audit standards persist across projects.
