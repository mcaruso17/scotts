## 2. Key Points & Insights

- Science, following Gary Becker's framing, is **"a web of relationships which rewards surprising discoveries and severely punishes lies and misrepresentation"** — a norm structure, not a single method.
- That same reward-and-punishment web is what unites the physical and the social sciences, so economics belongs inside it.
- Cunningham keeps meeting two recurring objections — "economics has no real Nobel" and "economics isn't a science" — and the essay is his answer to both.
- Econometricians earn their scientific standing by framing work around **policy problems and credible identification** (e.g., instrumental variables grew out of prior theory about endogeneity in supply and demand).
- He splits the field's core concerns into the **wealth of nations** (production, technology, growth) and the **wages of nations** (how output is allocated and divided, i.e., inequality).
- **Skill-biased technological change** is a "wages of nations" story — about technology's effect on *inequality* rather than on aggregate growth.
- **Updated Nobel pick:** his lead bet leans toward the **wages-of-nations / inequality** line of work (Autor, Katz, with Murphy), balanced against a **machine-learning-econometrics** line (Athey, Chernozhukov, with Newey) and an industrial-organization backup (Berry, Levinsohn, Pakes).
