## Key points / insights (one sentence each)

- Cunningham runs a long-form series arguing that AI agents like Claude Code
  meaningfully change how applied microeconomists do day-to-day research.
- His core worry is that the same tools that boost productivity can degrade research
  quality if researchers stop verifying, so verification is the central discipline.
- He built a "Referee 2" five-audit protocol (code audit, cross-language replication,
  replication-package check, output automation, and econometrics) to stress-test
  finished projects independently.
- Independence matters: audits are run in a fresh session by a Claude instance that
  never saw the original work, because the agent that built a pipeline cannot
  objectively critique it.
- Cross-language replication (e.g., R vs. Stata vs. Python, agreeing to 6+ decimals) is
  his practical test that a result is real rather than an artifact of one toolchain.
- He demonstrated live agentic research to the Federal Reserve Board (March 2026),
  including an agent that downloaded ~305,000 congressional speeches, classified
  immigration sentiment, and broadly replicated a prior study.
- The most recent identifiable standalone post is a workshop announcement — "Changing
  Market Conditions, Demand for Observational Causal Inference and Causal Inference I
  (Sept 27, Saturday) at Mixtape Sessions" — tying rising demand for causal skills to
  his teaching.
- His broader thesis: AI raises, not lowers, the returns to genuinely understanding
  identification and econometrics, because judgment is what verification requires.
