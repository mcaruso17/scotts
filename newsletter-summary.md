## Key Points / Insights (one sentence each)

- Connectors are the mechanism Cunningham sees for using Claude Code from his phone and extending it beyond his local computer into cloud services.
- The connector directory works like an "app store," with roughly 4,000 entries and growing, including ~451 under the "Code" category alone.
- MCP (Model Context Protocol) is an open standard—"a new kind of piping"—that lets AI applications securely connect to external data and tools and take real-time actions.
- Connectors split into vendor-published ones and user-created "Community" ones that are only lightly vetted, so the latter warrant a "buyer beware" posture.
- Financial-services connectors (Stripe, PayPal, QuickBooks, Gusto payroll, Dropbox) could let one person run a real small business, while direct banking access is notably absent, plausibly for security reasons.
- Connectors reach ordinary Claude-app users, not just Claude Code users, which Cunningham thinks is a large and under-served market in late 2026.
- He links AI-driven productivity to growth, noting the surge of "solopreneurs," a rising share of whom report \$1–10M in annual revenue with as little as one employee.
- For empirical research specifically, he is candid that the payoff of connectors is not yet obvious to him, even as he expects some cloud resources will eventually be reachable efficiently only through them.
- His long-sought "harness" is less a tool than a *staging of his own cognition*—a checklist-driven, design-principles workflow mapped to his book's end-of-chapter advice.
- He wants the harness to be deeply visual (interactive "flippable card" figures, a dashboard, even an Apple Vision Pro interface) to recover the "seeing" he lost by delegating code to agents.
- He reduces the aim of AI-assisted research to three requirements: **production, verification, and understanding**.
- He is honest about the friction—"drinking from a firehose" and having to "wrestle" Claude Code to work on his terms.
