- **The limits of "autonomous" empirical research** — capable, but not truly self-driving.

- **Treatment assignment mechanisms** — the design-based core of credible causal inference.

## Key Points / Insights

- AI agents can already "3-d print" a full empirical manuscript end-to-end in ~30 minutes, and that remains true six months on.
- Despite this, agents are not fully autonomous — more like lane-assist plus cruise control than a self-driving car.
- Research should pass through three "doors": **production**, **verification**, and **understanding**.
- **Production** is solved — agents can autonomously and endogenously choose and produce empirical projects (e.g., the Social Catalyst Lab's 1,000 automated program-evaluation papers).
- **Verification** is not simple replication; it is relative to a pre-stated target estimand and must also confirm that what *should not* have happened did not happen — which agents cannot fully self-check.
- **Understanding** is non-negotiable: the researcher is the "CEO" who delegates but must still know what was done, by whom, and what was found.
- The governing rule is a **zero-errors constraint**, not error *minimization* (which would tolerate "optimal" errors).
- Cunningham is building a narrow, checklist-driven **diff-in-diff harness** (inspired by Gawande's *Checklist Manifesto*), because experts skip steps more than beginners.
- Current checklist stages include **Packages** (version-pinned card) and **Bite** (exhaustively documenting the intervention's first-stage effect across space and time).
- The **"bite" stage should stay human-led** — it is where the project is conceived and where the treatment-assignment mechanism (rational choice, instruments, randomization, running variables) is diagnosed.
- Automate the mechanical production (maps, code, figures, tables); keep conception, verification, and judgment human-supervised.
