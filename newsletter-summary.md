## Main topics / themes

- **The economics of AI diffusion in research** — what happens to a
  researcher's advantage once everyone in the field has the same agentic tools.
- **Claude Code as a research instrument** — continuation of Cunningham's
  running series documenting practical, quantitative social-science use.
- **Returns to expertise** — where durable advantage lives when the cost of
  producing high-quality code falls toward zero.

## Key points / insights

- Positive surplus from an exclusive tool doesn't last: competition drives it
  toward the economist's **zero-profit condition** as the tool diffuses.
- He frames the future through Tyler Cowen's "what's the equilibrium?" lens —
  where everyone plays their best response to everyone else's best response.
- Since roughly mid-November, he reports Claude Code's speed, task range, and
  quality have no easily discernible upper bound.
- The cost of producing high-quality code has "basically fallen to zero,"
  shifting the scarce input from writing to **judgment and verification**.
- He builds custom tools that continuously send agents after his code —
  writing referee reports of his own work, stress-testing results, and
  replicating analyses across languages.
- The lasting edge moves to what the tool can't commoditize: good questions,
  credible identification, unique data, and rigorous checking.
