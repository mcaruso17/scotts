\begin{abstract}
\noindent This document summarises the main concepts behind the application of
artificial intelligence---and in particular agentic coding tools such as
\textbf{Claude Code}---to empirical research in the social sciences. It draws on
the ongoing public experiments and newsletter of economist Scott Cunningham,
whose ``Claude Code'' series documents what works, what fails, and the
principles that make AI a productive research collaborator rather than a
black box. The emphasis throughout is on \emph{reproducibility, verification,
and the AI as a thinking partner} rather than as an autonomous code generator.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
The arrival of capable agentic coding assistants has shifted AI from a tool that
\emph{answers questions} to one that \emph{performs research tasks}: writing and
running code, replicating papers, classifying text at scale, auditing results,
and producing publication-ready outputs. Claude Code---an agentic command-line
and desktop tool that can read a project, edit files, run programs, and iterate
on errors---sits at the centre of this shift for applied empirical work. The
central question is no longer ``can the model write code?'' but ``how do we
embed an AI agent into a research workflow so that the results are
\emph{trustworthy, reproducible, and verifiable}?''

\section{Core Philosophy: AI as a Thinking Partner}
The organising principle is that the most productive mode is \textbf{Claude as a
thinking partner}, not as an autonomous agent.
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.4em]
  \item The \emph{dialogue itself is the work}, not merely a delivery mechanism
        for outputs; the researcher steers iteratively and the agent executes
        when the next move is clear.
  \item Human judgement remains in the loop: the researcher decides direction,
        interrogates intermediate results, and refuses to ``trust the number''
        without verification.
  \item Autonomy is granted incrementally---tasks that are well specified can be
        delegated, while ambiguous or consequential steps stay under human
        control.
\end{itemize}

\section{Main Application Areas}

\subsection{Replication and Reproduction of Published Work}
A flagship demonstration replicated a \emph{PNAS} paper that used natural
language processing on roughly 200{,}000 U.S.\ Congressional speeches about
immigration. Claude Code re-implemented the pipeline, substituting OpenAI's
\texttt{gpt-4o-mini} for the original RoBERTa classifier.
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.4em]
  \item \textbf{Cost:} approximately \$11 in API charges for the full exercise.
  \item \textbf{Time:} about 1 hour of setup, 2.6 hours of processing (versus an
        expected 24 hours), and 1 hour of analysis.
  \item \textbf{Agreement:} roughly 69\% agreement with the original classifier,
        illustrating both the promise and the limits of cheap LLM-based
        classification as a substitute for purpose-trained models.
\end{itemize}
The broader lesson: AI agents dramatically lower the cost of \emph{reproducing}
empirical results, making replication a routine rather than heroic activity.

\subsection{Large-Scale Text Classification}
Reclassifying large corpora (e.g.\ legislative speeches) with inexpensive LLMs
is a recurring use case. The workflow shows how an agent can orchestrate data
acquisition, batching, API calls, cost tracking, and reconciliation against a
benchmark---tasks that previously required substantial engineering effort.

\subsection{Verification, Auditing, and Reproducibility}
A distinctive contribution is a suite of \emph{adversarial} verification
protocols (packaged publicly as ``MixtapeTools''):
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.4em]
  \item \textbf{Referee 2} --- a systematic audit protocol: code audits,
        cross-language replication (R / Stata / Python), directory-structure
        checks, output-automation audits, and econometric validation. Crucially,
        a \emph{fresh} Claude instance performs the review to preserve
        independence.
  \item \textbf{Blindspot} --- a perception audit that surfaces overlooked
        problems (``vices'') and missed opportunities (``virtues''), invoking the
        ``Shklovsky principle'' of restoring perception to work one has
        internalised.
  \item \textbf{Bibcheck} --- a multi-agent system that audits \texttt{.bib}
        bibliography files against canonical sources using parallel,
        narrow-focus agents rather than one comprehensive pass.
  \item \textbf{Split-PDF} --- a pipeline for acquiring and reading academic
        papers in chunks to avoid context overload.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Writing, Presentations, and Communication}
Tooling also targets research \emph{communication}: generating Beamer slide
decks with attention to rhetorical design, TikZ figures with collision detection
for visual accuracy, project scaffolding, and LaTeX compilation helpers.

\section{Key Design Principles}
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.6em]
  \item \textbf{Documentation equals permanent artifacts.} Persistent project
        context (e.g.\ a \texttt{CLAUDE.md} file) encodes conventions, data
        locations, and decisions so the agent behaves consistently across
        sessions.
  \item \textbf{Cross-software replication as quality control.} Re-deriving a
        result in a second language (R, Stata, Python) is a strong test of
        correctness.
  \item \textbf{Adversarial review requires structural separation.} Reviews are
        run in \emph{new} terminal sessions / fresh agent instances so the
        reviewer is not anchored by the author's reasoning.
  \item \textbf{Visual verification trumps numerical trust.} Plotting and
        eyeballing outputs catches errors that summary statistics hide.
  \item \textbf{Formal processes outperform intuition.} Explicit, repeatable
        protocols beat ad-hoc prompting for reliability.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Findings and Cautions}
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.4em]
  \item \textbf{P-hacking and incentives.} Experiments suggest AI agents lack the
        human career incentives that drive p-hacking: unless explicitly told to
        optimise for statistical significance, agents show little sign of it and
        will report null findings. (Cunningham has publicly revised earlier
        claims on this point---an example of treating these as live, falsifiable
        experiments.)
  \item \textbf{Truth and trust.} The strength and weakness of AI coding is the
        same property---fluent, confident output---so verification protocols are
        not optional add-ons but the core of credible AI-assisted research.
  \item \textbf{Cheap models, real limits.} Inexpensive classifiers (e.g.\
        \texttt{gpt-4o-mini}) make large studies affordable but only
        \emph{partially} agree with purpose-built models; agreement rates must
        be measured and reported, not assumed.
\end{itemize}

\section{Frontier and Speculation}
Cunningham also writes ``Claude Code fan fiction''---near-future scenarios
(months, not decades, ahead) about how AI agents may reshape scholarly
\emph{publishing} itself: not just writing manuscripts but compressing the cycle
of producing, reviewing, and reproducing empirical work. His recent talks (e.g.\
at the IMT School of Advanced Studies in Lucca, Italy, June 2026) present
research on the \emph{behaviour of AI agents in semi-autonomous empirical
research}---a research agenda in its own right.

\section{Practical Takeaways for Researchers}
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.6em]
  \item Start by treating the agent as a collaborator: specify the goal, inspect
        each step, and delegate only well-defined sub-tasks.
  \item Maintain a \texttt{CLAUDE.md} (or equivalent) so context and conventions
        persist across sessions.
  \item Build verification in from the start---cross-language replication,
        independent ``referee'' passes, and visual checks.
  \item Measure and report cost, runtime, and agreement with benchmarks; these
        are now part of the empirical record.
  \item Treat AI-assisted findings as falsifiable claims and be willing to
        publicly revise them.
\end{enumerate}
