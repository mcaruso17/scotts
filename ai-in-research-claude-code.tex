\begin{abstract}
This document summarises the main concepts emerging around the use of AI
agents --- and Claude Code in particular --- as tools for empirical research.
It is drawn from Scott Cunningham's ongoing ``Claude Code'' essay series on
\textit{Scott's Mixtape Substack}, anchored on the essay
\textit{``Stale code, deprecation, and the canon''} (21 July 2026). The central
thesis is that AI coding agents dramatically lower the marginal cost of
producing analysis, but that this abundance introduces a new failure mode ---
\emph{AI agent drift} --- which must be controlled by an explicit,
harness-like workflow that canonises a replicable pipeline and deprecates the rest.
\end{abstract}

\section{Context: what changes when an agent does the analysis}

AI coding agents such as Claude Code can now carry out substantial parts of an
empirical study: writing and editing data-cleaning and estimation code,
producing tables and figures, running robustness checks, and iterating rapidly
in a conversational loop with the researcher. This collapses the
\textbf{marginal cost of producing analysis} toward zero.

The economic consequence is not merely faster work. When output becomes cheap
and abundant, the binding constraint shifts from \emph{production} to
\emph{curation, verification, and organisation}. The researcher's comparative
advantage moves away from typing code and toward designing the workflow, stating
the estimand, and deciding which of the many agent-generated artifacts are
trustworthy enough to keep.

\section{The central problem: AI agent drift}

\begin{keybox}{Definition --- AI Agent Drift}
The tendency of an AI coding agent, over a long project, to silently
introduce changes, duplicate work, and let the analytical object mutate ---
so that the state of the analysis quietly diverges from what the researcher
believes it to be.
\end{keybox}

Two concrete symptoms motivate the whole framework:

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
  \item \textbf{Silent sample mutation.} On a long project the analytical
  sample changed from $N$ to $N+k$ observations with no explanation. The change
  was in the inter-temporal (not contemporaneous) results and was caught only
  because of a disciplined habit of producing ``beautiful tables and figures.''
  Nothing in the ordinary workflow was designed to catch it.

  \item \textbf{Program proliferation.} What the researcher assumed were edits
  to a single program had, in fact, become on the order of $5K$ near-duplicate
  programs with confusing naming conventions --- the classic copy-paste hazard,
  now generated by the agent, and putting even \emph{more} distance between the
  researcher and the work.
\end{enumerate}

Drift is dangerous precisely because agents are helpful and plausible: each
individual step looks correct when checked, yet the aggregate state slowly goes
wrong. Abundance without control produces \textbf{aggregate error}.

\section{The response: a rigid, staged harness}

The proposed antidote is an intentionally \textbf{rigid checklist} --- both a
statement of research \emph{design} principles and a direct reaction to agent
drift. It is embodied in a personal dashboard whose ``spine'' is the checklist,
grounded in established methodological guidance:
Don Rubin's \textit{``Design Trumps Analysis,''} Pedro Sant'Anna's
difference-in-differences checklist, and Guido Imbens's survey-article
guidance.

\subsection{The 12--14 step causal-inference checklist}

Each stage is a gate that must be ``closed'' (verified) before the project can
proceed, and each typically produces 2--3 exhibits (tables/figures).

\begin{description}[leftmargin=1.5em,style=nextline]
  \item[PKG] Enumerate every software package the project will use.
  \item[TARGET] State the causal parameter(s) explicitly using potential
    outcomes, populations, and weights; name each one clearly.
  \item[BITE] Document the treatment's ``bite'' --- real-world facts about the
    intervention (e.g.\ a time series of usage, a policy heat-map over a
    county shapefile).
  \item[COVARIATES] Select covariates needed for unconfoundedness, conditional
    parallel trends, or a factor model; produce imbalance tables and
    propensity-score plots. (Aided by a custom \texttt{/covariates} skill.)
  \item[ROLLOUT] Document treatment timing: a cohort count table with sample
    shares, plus a rollout figure via Yiqing Xu's \texttt{panelview}.
  \item[OUTCOME] Present the outcome's evolution in a form matching the
    intended analysis.
  \item[POWER] Run power calculations --- minimum detectable effect at
    $\alpha=0.05$, power $0.8$ --- with power curves and over-rejection
    diagnostics from Monte Carlo.
  \item[ESTIMATOR] Choose and estimate the estimator, flowing directly from
    TARGET, COVARIATES, and the reading of BITE.
  \item[EVENT] Produce event-study graphics (currently separated from, but
    trending toward merging with, ESTIMATOR).
  \item[FALSIFY] Run falsification tests --- outcomes and groups that should
    \emph{not} be affected by the treatment.
  \item[MECHANISM] Investigate candidate mechanisms explaining the estimates.
  \item[PIPELINE] Re-run everything to confirm replication; run \texttt{/referee2}
    and \texttt{/blindspot} checks (in practice run compulsively at every stage).
  \item[DELIVER] Storyboard findings into outlines, short papers, and other
    deliverables.
  \item[SIGN] Sign off once complete.
\end{description}

\subsection{Rituals, locks, and the status dashboard}

Within each stage the workflow uses fixed \textbf{rituals} that must be
completed to close the gate. Progress is made visible on a dashboard as a
left-to-right row of colour-coded circles, one per stage:

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
  \item \textcolor{green!55!black}{\textbf{Green}} --- closed: a sequence of
    verification steps gives confidence the stage is complete and correct.
  \item \textcolor{red!75!black}{\textbf{Red}} --- open: not yet closed. No
    project may ship with a red circle, so a red gate blocks completion.
  \item \textcolor{orange!85!black}{\textbf{Amber}} --- ``you are here'': the
    pulsing marker showing where to resume each day.
\end{itemize}

This turns a soft best-practice into a hard \textbf{harness}: a controlled
structure within which the agent operates, so that neither a ``forgetful
Claude'' nor the researcher's future self can lose track of the project's
true state.

\section{The canon metaphor: curation and deprecation}

The essay's organising metaphor is the formation of the biblical \textbf{canon}.
The early church faced a proliferation of letters and books with similar names
and similar-but-not-identical stories --- and had to run a process of
\emph{selection and curation} that decided what was included and what was
\emph{deprecated} (labelled ``apocryphal'' or ``heresy'').

\begin{keybox}{The analogy}
AI agents used for empirical research generate an abundance of files, figures,
tables, and code with similar names and similar-but-different stories.
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.2em,nosep]
  \item Some differences are \emph{benign} --- both artifacts can coexist
    (Mark vs.\ Matthew).
  \item Some differences are \emph{irreconcilable} --- a hard choice is forced
    (Mark vs.\ Thomas).
\end{itemize}
Researchers therefore need an explicit \textbf{criterion, process, and rule}
that selects the \emph{canon} --- the pipeline of code that produces the
exhibits supporting the final manuscript --- and deprecates the rest.
\end{keybox}

Before AI agents, researchers curated their work \emph{semi-consciously} through
coding etiquette, file-naming conventions, and version control (cf.\ Gentzkow
\& Shapiro's manual). Agents make that implicit curation impossible to leave
implicit: the drift toward multiplicity is now a natural, constant force, so
canonisation must become an \textbf{intentional, step-wise process}.

\section{Key takeaways for AI-assisted research}

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
  \item \textbf{Cheap production shifts the bottleneck to curation.} The value
    added by the researcher migrates from writing code to designing the harness
    and choosing the canon.
  \item \textbf{Drift is the defining failure mode.} Locally-correct agent
    steps can still produce globally-wrong state (mutated samples, duplicated
    programs); guard against the aggregate, not just the increment.
  \item \textbf{Design must precede analysis.} A pre-committed checklist
    (estimand first, estimator last) prevents the agent's fluency from
    substituting for research design.
  \item \textbf{Make state visible and gated.} Colour-coded, lockable stages
    ensure no project ships with an unresolved (red) step and that work is
    always resumable.
  \item \textbf{Canonise a replicable pipeline.} Decide explicitly what is
    canon vs.\ deprecated so the final exhibits are the ones you chose and they
    mean what you think they mean.
  \item \textbf{Custom skills encode expertise.} Reusable Claude Code skills
    (\texttt{/covariates}, \texttt{/referee2}, \texttt{/blindspot}) embed a
    researcher's methodological beliefs and checks into the workflow.
  \item \textbf{A general framework is coming --- but personalised.} A common
    ``harness-workflow-for-empirical-research'' will likely be articulated, yet
    comparative advantage will drive each researcher to tailor their own
    dashboard.
\end{enumerate}
