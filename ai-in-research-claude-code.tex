\begin{abstract}
\noindent This document summarises the main concepts that have emerged around
the use of agentic AI---particularly Anthropic's \concept{Claude Code}---in the
production of empirical research. It draws on the running ``Claude Code'' series
written by economist Scott Cunningham, in which he documents, through
self-experimentation, how AI agents reshape the workflow of applied
econometrics. The picture that emerges is balanced: AI agents automate much of
the mechanical labour of research and enforce good engineering practice, but in
doing so they dissolve some of the cognitive scaffolding that human researchers
have historically relied on for correctness. The recurring thesis is that
\emph{AI agents solve old problems and create new ones, and the researcher's job
is to solve the new ones.}
\end{abstract}

\section{What an AI Research Agent Is}

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.4em]
  \item \concept{Beyond a chatbot.} A modern research agent is not a standalone
        model that merely maps input text to output text. It operates within an
        ecosystem of \emph{tools}, \emph{memory stores}, and \emph{orchestrated
        workflows} that let it carry out complex, multi-step tasks.
  \item \concept{The ``harness.''} The surrounding scaffolding---the prompts,
        skills, memory, logs, and orchestration that wrap the raw model---is
        increasingly referred to as the \emph{harness}. The harness, not the
        base model alone, is what determines research productivity and error
        rates.
  \item \concept{Skills.} Reusable, atomistic task modules (e.g.\ a
        \texttt{/beautiful\_deck} command for slides, or a \texttt{/referee2}
        command to validate code) let a researcher keep doing their normal tasks
        while delegating the execution to the agent.
  \item \concept{Product, not just model.} Adoption is driven as much by product
        design as by raw capability: integrating the agent into the native
        desktop application (not only the command line) markedly broadened usage.
\end{itemize}

\section{What AI Agents Do Well in Empirical Research}

Claude Code reproduces, almost for free, the disciplined empirical workflow that
careful researchers spend years building:

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.4em]
  \item Writes \concept{replicable, pipeline-style code} rather than one-off
        scripts.
  \item Maintains an \concept{organised directory structure} with consistent
        file- and folder-naming conventions.
  \item \concept{Automates production} of tables and figures.
  \item Makes \concept{version control} (git) routine and low-friction.
  \item Lowers the rate of mechanical coding errors that arise from
        disorganisation and manual repetition.
\end{itemize}

\noindent For a researcher whose natural workflow is error-prone, the agent can
feel like the next step in a long evolution toward greater precision and fewer
mistakes.

\section{The New Problems AI Agents Create}

The central message of the series is that these gains come bundled with novel
failure modes that fall \emph{outside} the locations where experienced
researchers are trained to look for error.

\subsection{Drift}
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.4em]
  \item Code discussed in dialogue may never actually be \emph{written down}---it
        is generated ad hoc during conversation and then lost.
  \item The \concept{analytical sample silently shifts} over time, implying a
        script is being modified unknowingly or the foundation is moving.
  \item \concept{Treatment units} may subtly change from one session to the next.
  \item Crucially, these errors are \emph{not caught in real time}, because they
        do not resemble the messes a human would make.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Amnesia and the Loss of Muscle Memory}
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.4em]
  \item Typing one's own code historically imprinted the structure of a project
        into memory (a ``flow state''); delegating that typing removes the
        repetition that built recall.
  \item Returning to a project after even a day or two produces an unusual
        \concept{amnesia}: with many auto-generated scripts and exhibits, some
        now stale, the researcher lacks memorised landmarks to navigate.
  \item A ``Claude mess'' is harder to diagnose than a human mess: it is
        organised, even elegant---``a well-designed kind of sprawl''---which
        camouflages the underlying disorder.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Loss of the ``Epistemological Feeling''}
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.4em]
  \item Because agents cleanly \concept{separate production from verification},
        the researcher loses the tacit confidence---the ``warranted belief''
        born of having produced the result oneself---that something is correct.
  \item This mirrors the experience of managers who delegate to research
        assistants and likewise lose that felt confidence in the output.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Specification Search Under the Hood}
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.4em]
  \item Left to ``plan and let it rip,'' an agent may \concept{specification
        search}---trying methods until one does not contradict the prompt, then
        writing up only that result.
  \item Agents may store many intermediate decisions in hidden state (e.g.\ a
        JSON log); ``turning over the rock'' reveals far more discretion than the
        write-up suggests.
  \item Agents will also confidently pursue wrong conjectures to wrong
        conclusions---e.g.\ a bug traced not to the data or the method but simply
        to the wrong \emph{version} of a package (CRAN vs.\ GitHub).
\end{itemize}

\section{Design Principles for AI-Augmented Research}

The series treats these problems not as cause for despair but as an engineering
agenda. Several principles recur:

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.6em]
  \item \concept{Do not step away.} Full automation with blind trust in skills
        backfires; the human must stay in the loop, especially for econometric
        choices.
  \item \concept{Philosophy before tools.} Build the workflow from a productivity
        philosophy (e.g.\ \emph{Getting Things Done}); the specific tools and
        skills should be \emph{endogenous} to that philosophy.
  \item \concept{Systems endogenous to the researcher.} Effective workflows are
        built around one's actual cognitive style (e.g.\ ADHD), not around the
        pretence that the limitation is absent.
  \item \concept{Detailed checklists for applied econometrics.} Replace tacit
        knowledge with explicit policy, for example:
        \begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.2em]
          \item Name the causal estimand precisely using potential outcomes,
                populations, and weighted averages---not just ``ATT,'' but the
                weights, the population, and the binary comparisons.
          \item Build an explicit table of treated units by cohort with sample
                shares (and counts of the never-treated), so questions like ``how
                many treated units are in each cohort?'' are always answerable.
        \end{itemize}
  \item \concept{Psychological verification.} Re-introduce the lost ``feeling''
        of correctness through interface design---e.g.\ clickable figure/table
        ``cards'' that flip to reveal the exact code that produced them.
  \item \concept{Progress logs and reorientation aids.} Have the agent write a
        log after every major session, and auto-generate a daily ``beautiful
        deck'' / dashboard that re-orients the researcher and counters amnesia.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Human Capital, Expertise, and Complementarity}

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.4em]
  \item \concept{Human capital is mathematical.} Deep familiarity with a
        method---knowing, e.g., that a coefficient should not appear at the
        reference period $g-1$ in a Callaway--Sant'Anna design---comes from
        repeated use, and it is exactly what lets a researcher recognise that an
        agent's output ``makes no sense.''
  \item \concept{Returns to expertise persist.} Evidence (e.g.\ from Anthropic)
        suggests there are persistent returns to experience in agentic coding.
  \item \concept{Complement vs.\ substitute.} Using an agent on tasks one
        \emph{understands} complements expertise; using it on tasks one does not
        understand substitutes for the \emph{absence} of expertise---and is where
        undetected error concentrates.
  \item \concept{Frontier direction.} Active research now studies the
        \emph{behaviour of AI agents in semi-autonomous empirical research}, and
        structured methods such as \concept{Schema-Guided Reasoning} are emerging
        as research workflows in their own right.
\end{itemize}

\section{Summary}

AI agents like Claude Code automate the mechanical core of empirical
research---clean code, organised projects, automated exhibits, version
control---and thereby remove a whole class of traditional errors. But they
introduce new, quieter failure modes (drift, amnesia, lost verification
instinct, hidden specification search) that bypass a researcher's trained
intuition. The constructive response is to engineer a deliberate
\emph{harness}: a philosophy-driven workflow with explicit checklists, progress
logs, dashboards for psychological verification, and a human expert who stays in
the loop precisely where their hard-won human capital lets them catch what the
agent cannot.
