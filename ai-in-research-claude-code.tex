\begin{abstract}
\noindent This document summarizes the main concepts surrounding the use of artificial
intelligence---and in particular agentic coding assistants such as \textbf{Claude Code}---in
the conduct of empirical research. Drawing on Scott Cunningham's evolving ``workflow harness''
for applied econometrics, it organizes the ideas into an economic framing of AI-assisted
production, the central tension between \emph{production} and \emph{verification}, and the
practical scaffolding (memory, narrative, beauty, and checklists) needed to preserve research
quality when much of the cognitive output is generated by machines.
\end{abstract}

% ===============================================================
\section{Why AI Agents Matter for Research}
% ===============================================================
AI coding agents have moved from autocomplete tools to collaborators capable of carrying out
multi-step research tasks: writing and running analysis code, cleaning data, producing tables
and figures, drafting prose, and critiquing results. The practical consequence is a change in
the \emph{production technology} of knowledge work.

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Dialogue-driven work.} Research increasingly proceeds through conversation,
        reflection, and soliciting opinions from the agent, rather than through line-by-line
        manual coding.
  \item \textbf{Separation of production from verification.} The agent can \emph{produce}
        cognitive output that the researcher must still \emph{verify}---and these two acts are
        no longer performed by the same hands.
  \item \textbf{Tool-augmented capability.} Agents like Claude Code combine reading/writing
        files, running shell commands, searching code, and invoking specialized sub-routines
        (custom ``skills'' or audit passes) into a single research loop.
\end{itemize}

\section{An Economic Framing: The Flattened Isoquant}
% ===============================================================
A central idea is that AI agents have \textbf{``flattened the isoquant''} for cognitive output.
Model the production of research output $Y$ as a function of human hours $H$ and machine hours
$M$:
\[
  Y = f(H, M).
\]
When the isoquant for a fixed output level is (approximately) linear, human and machine time
become close substitutes. In the limit, a manuscript good enough to be \emph{submitted} to a
journal can be produced with machine time alone (though submission is not the same as
publication).

\paragraph{The incentive to reduce human time.} Because the opportunity cost of the
researcher's time is positive while the marginal cost of additional machine time is roughly
zero (subscription pricing rather than per-token), the natural tendency is for human time $H$
to fall. The key question becomes: \emph{how far should it fall?}

% ===============================================================
\section{Three Zones of AI Use}
% ===============================================================
Cunningham proposes thinking about the level of human attention $H$ relative to two reference
points: $H^{*}$, the pre-AI baseline, and $\bar{H}$, a personal \textbf{reservation time}.

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{@{}lll@{}}
\toprule
\textbf{Zone} & \textbf{Condition} & \textbf{Interpretation} \\
\midrule
Baseline        & $H = H^{*}$              & Pre-AI level of human attention. \\
Safe zone       & $\bar{H} < H < H^{*}$    & Less human time, yet \emph{more}/better output and learning. \\
Depreciation trap & $H < \bar{H}$          & Output produced but not understood; verification fails. \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Safe zone.} The researcher reclaims valuable time while still learning more
        and producing better work.
  \item \textbf{Depreciation trap.} Below the reservation time, the researcher generates output
        they cannot vouch for. Evidence cited includes experiments showing an agent will
        \emph{specification-search} when given even minimal discretion.
\end{itemize}

% ===============================================================
\section{Human Capital Depreciation and Verification}
% ===============================================================
The danger of pushing $H$ too low is a feedback loop:
\[
  \text{Cognitive output} \;\leftarrow\; \text{human capital}
  \;\leftarrow\; \text{prior attention} \;\leftarrow\; \text{human time } H.
\]
Human capital is increasing in attentive time. Once the isoquants flatten, output can be
produced \emph{without} attention---and therefore without building the human capital required
to \emph{verify} that output. The ``feeling of knowing'' that traditionally accompanied research
erodes, approaching zero as machine production rises and human attention falls.

\paragraph{A multiplicative production technology.} Borrowing from Shockley (1957), verified
cognitive output can be modeled as a Cobb--Douglas, multiplicative combination of several
inputs $F_1,\dots,F_8$:
\[
  Y_{\text{verified}} = \prod_{i=1}^{8} F_i .
\]
Because the inputs multiply, a near-zero value on any single factor collapses the whole. The
formulation also links \emph{supply} (production by the researcher) with \emph{demand}
(verification by editors and referees), making verified output an equilibrium rather than a
one-sided act.

\section{Zero Errors as a Constraint, Not a Goal}
% ===============================================================
A guiding design principle is the treatment of errors:

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Zero errors as a \emph{goal}} (a choice variable) leads to ``constrained error
        minimization,'' which can conclude that some non-zero level of error is optimal.
  \item \textbf{Zero errors as a \emph{constraint}} forbids that conclusion outright. It instead
        \emph{dictates the shape of the research}: which teams to use, which skills to invest in,
        which to abandon. Output quantity and quality then vary---possibly increasing in the long
        run, since errors can jeopardize a career.
\end{itemize}

The shift matters because errors arise under uncertainty, not intention; a workflow must
therefore \emph{re-introduce knowing}, not merely produce.

\section{The Workflow Harness: Four Pillars}
% ===============================================================
To keep research within reach despite heavy AI use, the proposed ``harness'' is built around a
dashboard and a checklist that store the project's memory and make its state visible.

\subsection{Pillar 1 --- Amnesia as the Default}
The workflow assumes the researcher begins each day in a state of ``amnesia,'' having no reliable
memory of prior agentic work. Rather than fighting this, the harness is designed \emph{around}
it---externalizing memory so the work can be re-entered each day.
(Metaphor: \emph{50 First Dates}---love, like the commitment to a project, persists even when
day-to-day memory does not; a daily practice of revisiting pictures and records restores it.)

\subsection{Pillar 2 --- Narrative Jogs Memory}
Understanding is encoded as \emph{stories}---e.g., understanding the biography of econometrics
(Ashenfelter, Card, LaLonde, Angrist, Currie, Heckman, Imbens, Rubin, Abadie, Duflo) to
understand its mathematics. The dashboard therefore includes outlines and evolving narratives
that interpret results and link material over time toward the eventual paper.

\subsection{Pillar 3 --- Beauty Makes You Look}
Beautiful dashboards, tables, figures, and an automatically generated daily ``deck'' (a
``rhetoric of decks'' philosophy) keep the researcher engaged. Staying on the page longer
sustains the constant, informed state needed for ongoing verification.

\subsection{Pillar 4 --- Checklists Save Lives}
A formalized 9--10 point checklist---modeled on surgery and aviation---gates the project:
\begin{itemize}
  \item Each checklist stage holds figures, tables, HTML, and descriptions of findings/checks.
  \item Work does not advance to the next stage until the current one is \emph{entirely} complete.
  \item Nothing in a stage ``exists'' until it has been audited (e.g.\ via a \texttt{/referee2}
        pass) and confirmed \emph{replicable}.
  \item The checklist doubles as the data appendix / replication package journals request on
        acceptance.
\end{itemize}
Checklists exist because the agent does not think, reason, or remember; it tends to
\textbf{drift}, and dialogue-driven use can widen the gap between what is asked and what is done.

% ===============================================================
\section{Practical Guidance for AI-Assisted Research}
% ===============================================================
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textbf{\arabic*.}]
  \item \textbf{Know your reservation time.} Do not let attentive human time fall below your
        personal $\bar{H}$, even though its exact value is unknowable.
  \item \textbf{Externalize memory.} Assume amnesia; persist project state in dashboards,
        outlines, and checklists rather than in conversation.
  \item \textbf{Separate but re-couple production and verification.} Build explicit audit steps
        that confirm replicability before output is trusted.
  \item \textbf{Make zero errors a hard constraint.} Let it reshape team, tooling, and skills
        rather than treating it as something to optimize against.
  \item \textbf{Use structure against drift.} Stage-gated checklists keep agentic exploration
        bounded and resolvable.
  \item \textbf{Invest in engagement.} Narrative and visual beauty are not decoration---they
        sustain the attention that verification requires.
\end{enumerate}

% ===============================================================
\section{Key Takeaways}
% ===============================================================
\begin{itemize}
  \item AI agents flatten the production isoquant, letting machine time substitute for human
        time in generating research output.
  \item The real risk is not that machines do the work, but that researchers \emph{cannot
        remember or verify} it---eroding human capital and the ``feeling of knowing.''
  \item Productivity can rise even as human time falls, but only within a ``safe zone'' above a
        personal reservation threshold.
  \item A durable AI research workflow treats \textbf{zero errors as a constraint} and supports
        it with externalized \textbf{memory, narrative, beauty, and checklists}.
\end{itemize}
