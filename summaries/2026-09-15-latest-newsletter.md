## Main topics / themes

- **Changing market conditions for empirical researchers** — how AI/agentic
  tooling is reshaping demand for quantitative and causal-inference skills.
- **The enduring (arguably rising) value of *observational* causal inference**
  — design-based identification as the durable, hard-to-automate skill.
- **Workshop promotion** — a new *Causal Inference I* cohort begins
  **Saturday, September 27** via Mixtape Sessions.
- **Ongoing context:** the running "Claude Code for quantitative social
  scientists" series and AI-in-research thread that has run through 2026.

## Key points / insights (one line each)

- AI agents are commoditizing the *mechanical* parts of empirical work (coding,
  cleaning, boilerplate estimation), shifting the premium toward research
  design and identification judgment.
- Observational causal inference — deciding *what* comparison identifies a
  causal effect — is precisely the expertise agents cannot yet supply.
- The *Causal Inference I* session is a 4-day, design-based sequence grounded
  in the Neyman–Rubin potential-outcomes model.
- Coverage includes randomization inference, independence/selection, matching,
  regression discontinuity, and instrumental variables.
- The framing is that "changing market conditions" *increase*, not decrease,
  the returns to knowing causal inference deeply.
- Mixtape Sessions remains positioned as low-cost, globally accessible causal
  inference training.
